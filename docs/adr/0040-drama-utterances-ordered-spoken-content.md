> "Dialogue and voiceover may interleave within the same scene" has been superseded by `docs/adr/0089`: dialogue and voiceover cannot coexist in one scene, and coexistence blocks generation; voiceover is delivered via narration voice-over or post-production dubbing. "Final-cut subtitles estimate duration from speech rate and allow gaps" is likewise superseded by `docs/adr/0089`: subtitle drafts are split per sentence and laid out sequentially within the outer bounds in proportion to reading units, and speech rate takes no part in subtitle timing. This ADR's decisions on ordered utterances as the single source of truth for spoken content, `source_text`, and the one-way constraint of speech volume on duration remain in effect.

# Drama spoken content is unified into ordered utterances, replacing the dialogue/voiceover dual fields

Drama's spoken content (character dialogue + voiceover) is unified into one **scene-level, ordered discriminated-union list** `scene.utterances: list[Utterance]`, each item `Utterance{kind: "dialogue"|"voiceover", speaker: str|None, text: str}`: insertion order is temporal order (expressing which comes first between dialogue and voiceover within a scene), and `kind` determines downstream routing (dialogue goes into the video YAML for the provider to generate a lip-synced audio track; voiceover is not used as a video prompt and is left for subtitles and future TTS; the criterion is whether the line belongs to a specific speaking character — voiceover carries only speaker-less narration/commentary), and the single source of truth for spoken content is utterances (`dialogue` is not embedded in `video_prompt`). This single sequence is the common foundation for three downstream consumers — final-cut subtitles are derived from it, the constraint of a scene's speech volume on its duration is estimated from it, and script_plan's structured output targets it; if spoken content were scattered across several unordered fields, subtitles would have no source, cross-type ordering would be lost, dialogue volume would decouple from duration, and novel narrative content could only be stuffed into the visuals and get distorted. Accompanying measures: voiceover for novel sources is opened up with restraint (the prompt gives context for creative judgement, gives no rules or category allow-list, and does not frame it as a fallback; screenplays are extracted verbatim); a scene-level `source_text` (a verbatim excerpt of the source, analogous to storytelling's `novel_text`, but purely a traceability anchor and never read aloud) supports human review, single-scene regeneration, and locating distortion; final-cut subtitles derive `subtitle_spans` from utterances (duration estimated from speech rate, placed sequentially, gaps allowed, not stretched to fill the scene) and land in Jianying (CapCut); the scene's speech volume takes a **one-way constraint** on duration (dialogue only pushes duration up, never pulls it down): at save time, an estimated speech duration exceeding duration only warns and does not block; at generation time, script_plan's duration guidance additionally steers the model to choose for each scene a tier no lower than the estimated spoken duration; duration is still driven by the visuals and is not mechanically rewritten.

## Considered Options

- **Keep 0036's dual fields and only add cross-type ordering (sequence numbers / order references)**: small change and the video YAML is untouched, but spoken content stays scattered in two places, the LLM has to keep two structures consistent, and subtitles / duration / structuring each still have to stitch two sources together; ordering is a bolt-on rather than a single source of truth.
- **Discriminate implicitly by presence of speaker (speaker=None means voiceover)**: saves a field, but "null as a semantic carrier" is obscure and easily confused with an empty string, and LLM structured output without an explicit enum is unstable. An explicit `kind` was chosen, consistent with the existing discriminator style of `ReferenceResource.type` / `AdUnitReference.type`.
- **Keep novel voiceover always empty**: preserves drama's "show, don't tell" purity, but narrative passages that can't be acted out within a single drama project could only be stuffed into the visuals and get distorted; opening it up with restraint and letting the LLM judge by context fits practice better.

## Consequences

- `lib/script/script_models.py`: `DramaScene`'s spoken content is `utterances` (`Utterance{kind, speaker, text}`, with a `kind ⇄ speaker` check: dialogue ⇒ speaker non-empty, voiceover ⇒ speaker is None), plus a scene-level `source_text`. Generic extras' speakers (`老人甲` (Old Man A)) are filled with the source's wording, do not enter characters_in_scene, and have no mechanical check. Whether `VideoPrompt.dialogue` is kept depends on whether narration / ad consume the field: if neither does, the shared model no longer carries dialogue; if one does, drama uses a dialogue-free variant.
- Existing data goes through "migrate on read" (`model_validator(before)`, modeled on `LEGACY_DROPPED_FIELDS`): old scripts' `video_prompt.dialogue` + `voiceover` are combined into `utterances` and the old fields stripped (only `extra="forbid"` + a "no worse" guard keeps this from erroring); old data has no interleaving information and mixed scenes were rare anyway, so the merge order is a deterministic best-effort (dialogue segment first, voiceover segment after), without pretending to restore the original.
- `lib/project/data_validator.py`: validates the utterances structure and `kind ⇄ speaker`, and `source_text`; adds a **warning** for "estimated speech duration > duration × tolerance" (following the "warn only, don't block" rule for ad total-duration drift). Speech rate has a single, adjustable source of truth (varies by language, and can be overridden at project level), shared with subtitles, not hard-coded.
- Speech volume on duration is a **directionally symmetric one-way constraint**, and two places consume the same speech-rate source of truth (`lib/speech/speech_rate.py`; precedence is project-level override > `source_language` language default > global default; overrides are layered only within the source of truth, and consumers do not each read project.json fields): at save time, a **one-way upper-bound warning** (the previous item: `lib/project/data_validator.py` only warns when the estimate exceeds tolerance, does not block or rewrite); at generation time, a **one-way lower-bound soft guidance** (the drama script_plan duration-constraint prompt in `lib/prompts/prompt_builders_script.py` steers the model to choose for each scene a tier **no lower than** that scene's estimated spoken duration from utterances; when speech exceeds the longest tier, the longest tier is taken and the save-time warning still backstops by exposing it). Both only push duration "up" and never "down": visuals / gaps can push duration past the lower bound, no mechanical logic rewrites the duration the model chose, and the "visual-driven + gaps are legitimate" position stands.
- `server/services/presentation/jianying_draft_service.py`: drama is registered as a subtitle mode and derives `subtitle_spans` from utterances (reusing the existing span rendering and the single-subtitle fallback); the "single field" model of `_SUBTITLE_TEXT_FIELDS` does not apply to drama and is replaced by an utterance→spans builder.
- How the script_plan / prompt_authoring pipeline produces utterances (structured script_plan, pass-through in prompt_authoring, web review gate) is covered in ADR 0041.
