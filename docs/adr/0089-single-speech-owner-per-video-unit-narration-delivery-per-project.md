# A video generation has only one speech owner, and voice-over narration does not go into the video model; narration delivery is a project setting and does not affect video generation

The content unit of a video generation (narration segment, drama scene, ad shot or video unit) mechanically derives a single speech owner from its speech entries: dialogue only, voice-over only, or no human voice. Dialogue (spoken lines, inner monologue, a character's off-screen commentary) is carried by the video model's native voice; voice-over as a whole does not go into the video prompt and is delivered by separate narration voice-over or external post-production dubbing. Dialogue and voice-over coexisting in the same unit is a violation that blocks generation: when a manual edit would make a unit like this, saving is refused; such units produced by machines or brought in by migration are flagged on write as needing re-planning or put into a draft awaiting repair; regardless of origin, they cannot be generated until fixed, and downstream does not automatically split, move or rewrite speech content. The speech owner is not persisted; it is recomputed every time from the body text or speech entries (`lib/speech/speech_composition/`).

**One unit corresponds to one video request, and voice ownership must be settled before the request.** A video model can only "act out" what characters say: if voice-over is written into the prompt, the model either has a character on screen lip-sync it, or randomly generates a voice that belongs to nobody, and neither can be stripped out afterwards. When dialogue and voice-over are mixed in one unit, no delivery method can satisfy both: handing it to the video model acts out the voice-over wrongly, and handing it to TTS loses the characters' native voices. Stopping mixing at the planning stage is the only place that needs no post-production patching.

**Narration delivery is a required project setting.** Its values are "TTS voice-over" or "post-production dubbing"; it is chosen in the project creation wizard, defaults to post-production dubbing, and can be changed at any time afterwards in the project settings. When TTS is chosen, the project must specify a TTS model, and the voice and voice-over speaking rate are also written into the project: they are pre-filled from global defaults at creation, and later changes to the global defaults do not affect the project. In TTS projects, ArcReel generates narration voice-over for units containing voice-over; in post-production dubbing projects, ArcReel produces no sound for voice-over, and the voice-over text is delivered with the subtitle draft for the creator to dub after delivery.

**Narration delivery does not affect video generation.** Both kinds of project request video by the arranged duration, which is estimated when writing the script from the voice-over text at the arrangement speaking rate; this is already the alignment point between picture and narration. Requested video duration, admission, quoting and post-generation checks do not read the delivery method, and the videos generated for the two kinds of project are identical. Narration voice-over is not a precondition for generating video: it belongs to the editing stage and is generated "fill in what is missing" as the first step of the agent's first automatic editing pass, and the creator can also generate it manually at any time; choosing TTS for a project counts as consent to these calls, with no separate confirmation. When the measured narration is longer than the picture, the edit timeline handles it: narration can extend onto following clips (`docs/adr/0090`); if it still does not fit, adjust the picture on the edit timeline, or shorten the voice-over text and regenerate that voice-over. The voice-over speaking rate has only one project-level value and cannot be adjusted per video unit. Voice-over text is the author's content, and shortening it during editing requires the creator's confirmation; confirming the rewrite counts as consent to regenerating its voice-over.

**Deliverables are distinguished by narration version.** A TTS project's final cut and Jianying (CapCut) draft can come in two versions, "with narration" and "without narration", which coexist without overwriting each other, defaulting to with narration; post-production dubbing projects have only the without-narration version. The variant in the artifact identity reuses `use_tts` / `post_production`.

## Explicitly rejected

- **Having the video model speak the voice-over**: the model would act out voice-over as dialogue of on-screen characters or as an ownerless voice, mixed into the native audio track inseparably.
- **Automatically splitting units downstream where dialogue and voice-over are mixed**: split points, duration allocation and picture ownership are all creative judgements, and the two parts a machine cuts out are often both invalid; block and point to the location, and let the planning layer or the author handle it.
- **Choosing the delivery method per generation request**: the same project would end up with material generated both ways; every entry point would have to ask again and pass it again, and a single missed pass would silently fall back to the default; batch admission would also need the extra constraint "only one method per batch". The delivery method is essentially a production decision for the project, not an option of a single request.
- **Storing only a default on the project while requests can still override it**: keeps all the problems of per-request choice, just asking one time fewer.
- **Using the measured duration of narration voice-over as a lower bound for the requested video duration**: TTS would be forced to become a prerequisite step for video, crossing tiers would require reconfirming cost, video models with fixed durations could not be used in TTS projects, and video generation would fork by delivery method. The arranged duration is already aligned by speaking rate, and handling the residual length difference during editing is cheaper.
- **TTS settings inheriting global defaults**: the generation basis of narration voice-over includes provider, model, voice and speaking rate, so changing a global default once would make every project's narration voice-over stale.
- **Truncating or speed-changing already generated narration voice-over to fit the picture**: this would damage the voice-over itself; when shorter narration is needed, regenerate it, or adjust the picture on the edit timeline.
- **Overriding the voice-over speaking rate per video unit**: the built-in TTS backend does not support a rate parameter, so the setting would have no effect under the default configuration; speeding up sounds close to speed-changed playback; and an override value would add another dimension to the narration voice-over's generation basis, with no good answer for how to treat existing overrides after the project speaking rate changes.

## Consequences

- The system does not perform voice separation on the provider's native audio track, nor adjust its volume automatically; the original audio volume comes only from editing decisions on the edit timeline (`docs/adr/0090`). The with-narration version overlays narration voice-over as an independent audio track on top of it; the without-narration version adds no narration track. Without speech recognition, the system only guarantees that planning and requests do not actively mix the two; it does not guarantee that the waveform the provider actually generates has no unexpected human voice.
- Subtitles are a mechanical draft: dialogue uses the actual video duration as the outer bound; voice-over uses the actual voice-over duration as the outer bound in the with-narration version and the actual video duration in the without-narration version. Each text is split into multiple subtitles at sentence-ending punctuation (Chinese at `。！？…`, English and Vietnamese at `.!?` followed by whitespace), and laid out in order within the outer bound together with the unit's other texts, proportionally by reading units (characters for Chinese, words for English and Vietnamese), with no claim of sentence-level alignment; when precise alignment is needed, hand it to Jianying (CapCut) for fine-tuning.
- Changing the delivery method does not make any existing artifact stale: the delivery method is not part of the video's generation basis, and subtitles, presentation, final cut and Jianying (CapCut) draft each have their own artifact identity per narration version. After switching from post-production dubbing to TTS, pictures shorter than the narration are reported by the edit timeline's checks and video is not regenerated automatically; after switching from TTS to post-production dubbing, existing narration voice-over is kept. Changes to voice-over text only invalidate the voice-over, subtitles and presentation that depend on it, not the video (`docs/adr/0062`, `docs/adr/0064`).
- There is no separate delivery-method selection step in the workflow; narration voice-over for TTS projects appears in the editing stage.
- When migrating existing projects, projects whose artifact manifest already contains narration voice-over artifacts and from which a TTS model can be resolved are classified as TTS projects, with the model, voice and voice-over speaking rate resolved at that time written into the project; the rest are classified as post-production dubbing projects.
- The body text of narration/commentary segments is required and is the voice-over itself, so segments cannot carry dialogue: what a character says is reported by the narrator in the body text, not written separately as dialogue. If the body text and the dialogue are the same sentence, or the body text quotes that line of dialogue, it is treated as mixed and not deduplicated by machine: deduplicating to dialogue would leave the segment without voice-over, and deduplicating to voice-over would require rewriting speech content.
- The TTS generation base count is one per unit containing voice-over in a TTS project (`docs/adr/0010`); the premise in `docs/adr/0040` that the same scene can interleave dialogue and voice-over no longer holds.
