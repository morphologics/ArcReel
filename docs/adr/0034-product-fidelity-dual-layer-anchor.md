# Product fidelity dual-layer anchor: the user's original images are the acceptance anchor; a standardised product sheet is an optional derived reference

Products in a promotional final cut must be faithful to the real product (distortion carries a risk of false advertising), but product images uploaded by users commonly have messy backgrounds, poor lighting and missing angles. The existing three asset types (character/scene/prop) use AI-generated sheets as the downstream consistency anchor, and for products AI redrawing means drift in logos, packaging text and material textures gets locked in at the source. Decision: product assets use two layers — the user's original images are mandatory and are always the fidelity acceptance anchor; a standardised product sheet is an optional, cleaned-up derived reference.

## Decision

- **The original images are the anchor**: a product asset holds multiple user-uploaded original images (`reference_images`), which are the acceptance standard for "the product in the final cut is faithful to the real product"; this reverses the earlier restriction that "user ad-hoc uploaded reference images are out of scope", and the original images go directly into downstream generation references.
- **The product sheet is optional and has the same lifecycle as other sheets**: the product image upload entry offers a "生成商品资产图" (Generate product asset image) checkbox, which triggers generation of a standardised multi-angle sheet (through the asset generation queue, reviewed/regenerated on the asset page); the agent workflow arranges for the user to review it before storyboarding begins. The sheet is an output, registered in the output inventory and judged into four states by the same rules — changes to the description, style or original images make it stale; stale is still usable and is not automatically regenerated (see `docs/adr/0062`). The drift from AI redrawing is absorbed by "the original images are always present and are the acceptance anchor": the sheet is only a cleaned-up derived reference, and whether the final cut is faithful to the real product is always checked against the original images.
- **Product reference injection is binary, with no "weak injection"**: on the storyboard-image-to-video path, shots are marked with `products_in_shot` (referenced by name), and non-empty means a product shot — product references are injected in full, ordered with absolute priority, with a high-fidelity instruction attached; atmosphere shots get zero product images. Reference images are strong conditioning; "give the image but ask it not to look too similar" is mechanically self-contradictory and causes drift; art-style unification is carried by the project-level style mechanism, not by product images. The reference-to-video path (`generation_mode=reference_video`) skips storyboarding; product reference images are resolved at execution time in order of the first mention of `@[商品名]` (`@[product name]`) in the video unit's body text and injected into the video request, by the same rules as other assets and not placed first (see `docs/adr/0064`).
- **Product fidelity carried in a single layer (storyboard-image-to-video path)**: on the storyboard-image-to-video path, product references are injected only at the storyboard generation stage and baked into the frame; product reference images are not appended a second time to the video request — the storyboard image is already the complete anchor for product fidelity, and repeated injection at the video layer is a redundant path for that goal.
- **Product is the 4th ASSET_SPECS entry**: the spec abstraction formally gains list-field capability (multiple original images, a list of selling points), a principled extension rather than a product special case; it does not enter the global asset library for now (incompatible with the single-image column model; cross-project reuse is future work).

## Consequences

- This is the first asset type centred on "multiple user-uploaded images" whose uploaded images go directly into downstream references; the tension between compression on upload save (normalize_uploaded_image's 2MB/q85 threshold) and anchor purity has been ruled in favour of keeping the originals: product original image uploads only validate decodability, keeping the original bytes and extension with no normalising compression (`server/routers/files.py`) — note this is a separate step from reference upload copy compression (ADR 0012, at send time, visually lossless tier).
- When a sheet exists, the downstream injection set is "sheet multiple angles + original images as backing"; the exact ratio is left to implementation tuning and not fixed at the design level.
- Product assets have no separate approval status field: the sheet's usability is answered by output freshness, and "whether the user has looked at it" is not a dimension in the data model. If the user is unhappy with the sheet, the way out is to regenerate it on the asset page, or not tick sheet generation and use only the original images.
